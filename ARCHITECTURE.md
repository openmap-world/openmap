# OpenMap architecture

OpenMap targets **World of Warcraft: Forever** (`_classic_beta_`, `## Interface: 16001`):
classic content on the retail 12.x engine. Its world map is the classic-era MapCanvas
map (`WorldMapFrame`, data providers, a pin frame-level manager); the reference source is
the `forever` branch of [Gethe/wow-ui-source](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source)
(`Blizzard_MapCanvas`, `Blizzard_WorldMap` with its `Camelot` overrides, and the retail quest
log, `Blizzard_UIPanels_Game/Mainline/QuestMapFrame`).

Some values are **secret** on this client (combat values, sometimes positions): never
compare, do arithmetic on or nil-test a value that may be secret. Read through `ns.Safe`.

## Layout

```
OpenMap/
  Core.lua        settings (OpenMapDB / OpenMapCharDB), events, Print, options helpers,
                  Look (fonts, panels), ns.Safe
  Data/           generated by tools/ (regenerate, don't edit) ... except Levels.lua
  Map/            the map (one module, "worldmap")
  Media/Textures/ masks and textures (made by tools/; NOTICE.md says from what)
  Init.lua        starts it at PLAYER_LOGIN; options; /openmap
tools/
  map-data/       Data/Reveal.lua, Data/Places.lua from the game's tables
  map-flights/    Data/FlightData.lua (the flight network)
  map-caves/      Data/Caves.lua + MapCave masks (Blizzard's cave / pre-instance maps)
  map-data/minimap.py  Data/Minimap.lua (the minimap tiles' file IDs)
  map-grade/      Data/Grade.lua + MapBand / MapTerritory / MapCore masks
  map-textures/   edge fades, parchment grain
  map-preview/    renders the stitched map out of game; bakes MapTerritory / MapZoneLand / MapLand;
                  sea.py, sea_world.py (the sea), gapfill.py (the gaps), banners.py
                  (Orgrimmar's banner and cut), tone.py (zone colours)
  map-art/        export.py / import.py (hand-cleaned world and continent art), border.py
  map-tuning/     align.py: measures each zone's art against the minimap (report only)
  offline-test/   loads the addon in a stubbed client (run.py, lint.py)
art-edits/        Blizzard's map art edited by hand (names painted out; the border)
docs/             development.md (how to work on it), testing.md (the in-game check),
                  releasing.md, maintaining.md, curseforge.md
```

The game's tables come from wago.tools (`https://wago.tools/db2/<Table>/csv?build=<build>`)
and its textures by FileDataID (`https://wago.tools/api/casc/<id>?download&version=<build>`).
Keep those caches outside the repo.

## The stitched map (`Map/Stitch.lua`)

Every zone map is drawn onto one canvas in Azeroth's (947) art pixels, placed by
`C_Map.GetMapRectOnMap(map, 947)`. Layers, bottom to top, each fading in by zoom:

- **sea**: the sea colour (`MapSeaField`), then Blizzard's sea paper (`MapSeaDark` /
  `MapSeaLight`) in crossfading octaves, so it keeps its size on screen as you zoom
  (`S.SetSea`). Past the world art's edges, the continents' own sea carries on (`MapSeaOut`).
- the **world** and **continent** art (wide feathered edges; their sea and continent names
  painted out by hand, `art-edits/`), drawn through its land (`MapBackLand`) and its sea
  (`MapSeaMask`). The world's open sea (the whirlpool, the ship) stays the ocean until well
  in; its coastal hatching goes sooner (`MapSeaCoast`). Over land it mutes once zones cover
  it. Along each continent's coast, Blizzard's wave strokes (`MapHatch` through
  `MapCoast`), finer octaves fading in with the zoom. Continent **land** stays land
  (`MapLand`: a smooth colour field of the continent art's land, its ink strokes and
  baked-in names left out; parchment tint and `MapGrain`), so regions without a zone
  map (Quel'Thalas, Gilneas ...) meet the zones as land.
- **gap fill** (`Data/Gaps.lua`, from `tools/map-preview/gapfill.py`): land no zone
  paints is blank: the zone maps' own parchment (`MapPaper`, two sizes that never line up)
  through `MapGapLand`, crisp along the coast. Over that, Blizzard's own maps of the land that
  Forever's tables don't use (`Gaps.lua`'s `art`: the classic base maps of 18 zones, which
  paint the land round them, and Gilneas' base map), drawn from the client at their own
  resolution through `MapCoastArt`. It fades in with the zone detail. Nothing is painted in
  or blurred across: only Blizzard's art, or paper.
- **zone base art**, shown on the zone's territory (`MapTerritory`, `render.py
  --bake-territory`): as far as Blizzard painted it, its own land and, inside its frame,
  the sea off its coasts (Blizzard's hatching, shallows and islets, up to `TERR_SEA` off the
  continent's coast) and the land round it (the mountains and coasts its map draws of its
  neighbours' surroundings). Not another zone's own land, not the names it prints (the
  sea's, its neighbours'), not its compass roses, not where a classic map is drawn. Where
  several zones paint the same place their claims are shared out by how near their own land
  is, each fading at its frame, so one hands over to the next; past every zone's painting
  it fades into the paper. (`MapZoneLand` and `MapZoneBorder`, for darkening, are made from
  the zone's own territory: its land and shore.)
- **zone overlays** (the explored-area pieces, `Data/Reveal.lua`, explored ones from the
  client): a thin, wavy feather at the art's edge.
- **zone interiors**: the overlays again through `MapCore`, above every zone's parchment halo.
- **cities**: base art with a round feather, fading in near the zoom where they fill the view.
  Orgrimmar comes in sooner. Orgrimmar and Undercity are cut to their plan and their own name
  banner (`MapCityMask`), under their zone's banner for them (`MapBanner`; the zone's own art is
  drawn without it, `MapBannerHole`), which gives way to the city's own as it comes in.
- **names**: the continents' and seas' names, lettered live in the client's font with the
  art's halo (`Data/Labels.lua`, measured from the art), one of each, fixed on the world.
- **icons and lines**: pin sets (places, flight masters, townsfolk, group members,
  map pins, boat / zeppelin routes). Each pin hangs from an anchor placed in the art; the pin
  layer is on the map's view, not its canvas, so it never scales: the pins keep their size on
  screen and a zoom only moves them. A set with `build` makes its pins the first
  time it shows.

**Baked zones** (`Data/ZoneArt.lua`, `MapZones/`, from `render.py --bake-zones`): the zone
layers above (base art, overlays, their colour-match copies, the interiors, the gaps' finer
maps) composited offline onto the world in 512-px tiles at 16 px per world unit. At the
settings they're baked at (colour match and desaturation at their defaults, unexplored areas
not hidden) they're drawn instead of the live zone layers, a few dozen tiles where the live
layers are hundreds of masked textures, until the view passes the tiles' own pixels; then
the live layers take over (`Baked.FADE`, in screen px per tile px), tile by tile near the view.
Drawn at `1 - (1 - d)^2` for the zone detail's fade `d`, as strong as the live layers stacked.

Unexplored areas: each overlay piece is a shared `{ key, unexplored }` table, so
`S.ApplyGrade` can show, dim or hide them and `S.RefreshExplored` (on
`MAP_EXPLORATION_UPDATED`) flips the ones you've just explored. Dimming is a shade over the
zones' art and the close-up minimap (`shadeRoot`), through each zone's land without its painted
sea (`MapZoneLand`): all of a zone you haven't found any of, from every zoom, with its share of the
border land round it (`MapZoneBorder`, so neighbouring undiscovered zones darken as one), or each
unexplored piece through its own tiles' shapes.

Border colours are matched by a tinted copy of each zone drawn through its border-band
mask (`MapBand`) with the multipliers in `Data/Grade.lua`. Everything is placed once, in
world units on one root frame; `Layout(mapID)` only sets that frame's scale and corner for
whichever map's canvas Blizzard's map is showing. Layers that have faded out are hidden,
not drawn at alpha 0, and of the zones near the view only the tiles near it are shown (and
have their files loaded), against a box round the view that a pan redoes only near its edge
(`S.Cull`). A hidden zone texture (and a zone's live-layer masks, and the gap paper off the view)
lets go of its anchor until it shows again: every region hangs from root, and the game lays out
again everything anchored to a frame that moves, hidden or not, on each frame of a pan or zoom.
The sea's and the grain's textures are fixed on the world, so a pan lays them again only near
the edge of the box they cover.

## Hosting it in Blizzard's map (`Map/WorldMap.lua`)

On world, continent and zone maps: a pin frame level above the exploration overlays, zoom
levels from the whole world down (`ScrollContainer.CreateZoomLevels` hook), pan extents
covering the world (`CalculateScrollExtentsAtScale` replaced), wheel zoom at the cursor,
click to zoom in / out. Zoomed right out the world is centred; zooming in, the view gains
room past the world's edges, so the wheel zooms at the cursor from the first step. Once the
view has been still for a moment after the user moved it, `SetMapID` switches
to the zone or continent under the view centre and the view is put back exactly
(`SetView`), so Blizzard's and other addons' pins always belong to what's on screen. No
map switching in combat. Wheel and click zooms run our own animation (`Animate`, after
Blizzard's OnUpdate), so the point under the cursor stays put and the map can be dragged
mid-zoom. While stitched, Blizzard's own art for the host map is hidden (it's under ours),
and from the continents' zoom the hover highlight is the zone under the cursor. Other maps
(dungeons, battlegrounds) behave as Blizzard made them.

## The closest zoom (`Map/Close.lua`)

Fed the view (centre, half extents, zoom) on every pan and zoom (`S.SetView`):
- **minimap**: the game's minimap tiles (`Data/Minimap.lua`) for what's on screen, at
  `minimapMix` (45%) so the painted map leads, rising toward `minimapDeep` (its most) at the
  deepest zoom; from `minimapFrom` (20x; 1x: the whole world). Pooled, at most 64 tiles; further
  out, two smaller copies of it (`MapMiniLOD`, `Data/MinimapLOD.lua`, from
  `tools/map-preview/minimaplod.py`: 64 and 16 px a tile, in 256-px pieces) show instead, drawn
  coarse under fine under the tiles with strengths that add up to the mix, the finer taking over
  as fewer tiles are in view.
- **soften + grain**: a five-tap blur of the zones in view (four offset copies, built
  lazily), and `MapGrain` + `MapTooth` over the art in two crossfaded octaves so the grain
  keeps its size on screen.
- **underground**: caves and pre-instance areas (`Data/Caves.lua`, Blizzard's micro maps;
  `MapCave` masks drop their frames). One only surfaces when you're centred on it and it
  nearly fills the view; the surface dims. Entrance icons glide down into theirs.

## The rest of `Map/`

- `Extras.lua`: coordinates bar, zone levels and fishing skill on the hover label
  (`Data/Levels.lua`), its own fade while moving (`moveFade`), hidden town / city icons and filter reset
  button, and Questie's pins from neighbouring zones (HereBeDragons only shows a zone's own).
- `Flights.lua`: flight masters from `Data/FlightData.lua`; learned ones recorded at flight
  masters per character; click two for the route and a time (measured flights override
  the estimate).
- `Townsfolk.lua`: vendors, trainers, rares ... from Questie's NPC database at run time,
  built the first time a kind is turned on.
- `Panel.lua`: the OpenMap menu (the button in the map's bottom-left corner), in the map
  window's own frame: search, the actions (find me, map pins, the quest log beside the map,
  report a problem, options), the layer checklist with an icon each (and Questie's own
  map-icon setting), and the deepest zoom. A star on an action or a setting puts it on the
  bar beside the OpenMap button (favourites).
- `Routes.lua`: boat and zeppelin routes as evenly spaced dots along the real paths, in
  nested densities; Alliance blue, others red; quiet until the cursor comes near one.
- `Nav.lua`: find me, reopening where you left off, Ctrl-click map pins (and TomTom),
  search (zones, dungeons, flight masters), group members off the map underneath.
- `Window.lua`: move (title bar) and resize (a grip in each bottom corner) the windowed map to any shape,
  remembered; the maximized map fills the screen's width; the parchment border (nine pieces,
  `MapBorder`) round the view, seen only zoomed right out. Re-applied after Blizzard's panel layout, never by editing its
  layout tables (taint).
- `Report.lua`: beta feedback: circle the problem on the map, add a note, get a report
  code (`OM1|...`) to paste into a GitHub issue; `tools/map-preview/report.py` renders it.
- `Paint.lua`: map notes, for working on the map (`/openmap notes`): paint an area, say what's
  there (fix this, look into this, an idea), list them, export them all as text;
  `tools/map-preview/notes.py` renders each one. Saved under `paint`.
- `Debug.lua`: the debug overlay (`/openmap debug`): frame rate, a graph of frame times, the
  worst frame, what OpenMap's zoom and close-up updates cost, textures drawn.
- `Bench.lua`: `/openmap bench`, a fixed course timing the map (still, panning, zooming) at
  several zooms about the view, then each part of the map off in turn (`S.Parts`, and every
  addon's pins) at the slowest; the results in a box to copy and in `lastBench`. Map switching
  waits while it runs (`mod.benching`), and `S.bench` collects each timed part's milliseconds.
- `Compat.lua`: other world map addons alongside. While the map is stitched it hides their map
  art (High Fidelity World Map's minimap tiles, BetterMaps' terrain and caves, HiddenMaps'
  approach maps, Leatrix Maps' icons) and shows it again on other maps; sets OpenMap's zone label,
  coordinates CVars and map fade again after theirs; names the options of theirs that still fight
  OpenMap's window. `WorldMap.lua` ignores their view changes there (`InstantPanAndZoom` from another
  addon) and trims their zoom steps past OpenMap's deepest.
- `Gamepad.lua`: Forever's gamepad mode. Blizzard's own cursor on the map (`SoftCursor`) has
  no mouse-over, so what's under it (OpenMap's buttons, menu and map icons) is entered and left
  as the mouse would, and A presses it. `WorldMap.lua` reads that cursor wherever it reads the
  mouse's (`PadCursor`), zooms the stitched map with the right stick at a steady rate about it
  (Blizzard's adds a fixed scale a second), and makes A and X zoom in and out a map as clicks do.

## Ideas and open questions

- Dungeon interiors: Blizzard's art for every classic dungeon floor is in the client too
  (instance coordinates, not world): open them from the entrance's cave map.
- Other addons' pins (HandyNotes, GatherMate) only show on the map underneath, as Questie's
  did before `Extras.lua` widened it; the same trick could cover HereBeDragons generally.
- Translations: `ns.L` is in place and the panel, tooltips and newer options go through
  it; no translations exist yet, and the older option descriptions don't use it.

## Things that can break with game or addon updates

- **Blizzard's map**: `WorldMap.lua` replaces the scroll container's
  `CalculateScrollExtentsAtScale` (to pan over the whole world) and, while stitched, its
  `ResetZoom` (each open goes straight to the remembered view: one icon rescale, or none), and sets its scale and
  scroll fields directly (`SetView`: Blizzard's own pan-and-zoom drifts). `Extras.lua`
  swaps the area label's OnUpdate (to add zone levels). Everything else is
  `hooksecurefunc` or a data provider. Map switching waits out combat; the in-game check
  (docs/testing.md) opens the map in combat to catch taint.
- **Questie**: `Townsfolk.lua` reads Questie's NPC database through
  `QuestieLoader:ImportModule`, and `Extras.lua` widens which of Questie's pins show on the
  stitched map. Neither is a public API; both fail quietly (a chat line, no pins) if
  Questie changes.
- **Data sources**: everything in `Data/` comes from the game's tables except
  `Levels.lua`, which the client doesn't carry (zone ranges from the Warcraft Wiki, see the
  file), `Labels.lua` and `Banners.lua` (measured or traced on the art), and the placement of
  the maps in `Gaps.lua`'s `art` (from Mists of Pandaria Classic's tables, which still place
  them). Dungeon levels come from the game's group finder (`tools/map-data/build.py`).
- Spirit healers: no graveyard table in this build; they come from Questie's database.
